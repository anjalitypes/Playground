The 3-fold Challenge

Three questions defined the project

From day one, three questions defined this project. Two came from the design brief. The third came from the Product VP, who was concerned about timelines and proposed trading design quality for speed.

1.
How do we create an AI experience that feels familiar yet native to LitCharts?
2.
Can we build it in a way to be able to scale across future AI tools?
3.
Could we protect design quality without slowing down?

These questions shaped everything that followed.

Research

Understanding the AI tool landscape

I compared AI tools in the market to identify common UX patterns across the space:

  • Conversational chat interfaces were the norm — the AI spoke in the first person
  • Common logo types ranged from personified entities to sparkle-icons
  • Tool naming also showcased personification, copilot or including AI within the name

With this in mind, we settled on including "AI" in the name and using a sparkle for the logo to optimise user understanding of LitCharts' new AI tool.

Ideation

Challenging the brief

The specs originally called for a single Q&A interface. A running chat interface surfaced as the more familiar and scalable solution — I secured approval to change the direction.

In parallel, I studied micro-interactions in Codepen and Figma, building a toolkit that gave engineering the confidence to commit to motion design as a quality baseline.

Strategy

Solving for speed and scale

The VP's concern about quality vs. speed wasn't just an immediate problem — it pointed to a deeper one: how do you build AI features fast without rebuilding from scratch each time?

I proposed two things in parallel:

1.
CSS rules written directly into design specs, so engineers had everything they needed.
2.
A scalable React system that allowed us to ship faster with the next three AI tools.

One workflow solved the deadline. The other solved the next three.

Results

The highest-converting feature in LitCharts history

Ask AI became the highest-converting feature in LitCharts history, enabling subsequent AI tools and contributing to 40% revenue growth for 2024. Process-wise, the CSS specs made workflow innovation possible. The engineering manager, Alan Nguyen, shared:

"The CSS specs helped us create the most responsive feature to date and eliminated the need for javascript to handle responsiveness like some of our legacy features."
— Alan Nguyen, Engineering Manager
Reflection

The best outcomes came from collaboration

The Product VP raised concerns early, creating space to problem-solve together. Engineering's openness to CSS specs made workflow innovation possible. My direct report's eagerness to push past the initial brief is what surfaced a scalable interface.

Prototyping early and specifying details upfront meant we shipped faster and better.
