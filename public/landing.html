FAQ

Common questions

How is this different from just using an AI detector? +
Standard AI detectors compare text against statistical patterns of AI writing in general. AuthentiCheck compares the paper against that specific student's own writing, which means it detects deviations in their personal voice, vocabulary level, and sentence style — regardless of whether the content is technically "AI-like" in a broad sense.
Are my students' papers stored or logged? +
No. Both papers are held in memory only for the duration of your analysis session and are never written to disk or stored in any database. Once you close the tab or start a new analysis, the content is gone.
What file formats are supported? +
AuthentiCheck accepts .txt (plain text) and .pdf files up to 10MB each. Text is automatically extracted from PDFs. For best results, ensure the PDF contains selectable text (not a scanned image).
Can I use this as proof in a disciplinary proceeding? +
AuthentiCheck provides strong supporting evidence, not a definitive legal determination. We recommend using the analysis to inform a conversation with the student, and treating it as one piece of evidence alongside assignment context, submission timing, and interview with the student. Most schools require a holistic review before formal action.
What if the student improved significantly since the reference paper? +
Genuine improvement shows up differently than AI substitution. Natural growth tends to maintain consistent voice and stylistic patterns while adding vocabulary gradually. AI-generated text shows abrupt, wholesale shifts across vocabulary complexity, sentence structure, and argumentative patterns simultaneously — which is what AuthentiCheck detects.
How accurate is the AI probability score? +
In internal testing, AuthentiCheck achieves ~94% accuracy distinguishing student-written from AI-generated papers when a representative reference sample is provided. Accuracy improves with a longer, more representative reference paper (500+ words is ideal). Always use the score as a starting point for further investigation, not a final verdict.
